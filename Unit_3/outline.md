> **Scope Boundary (Unit 3 vs. Unit 5):** Unit 3 focuses exclusively on mechanical work, power, gravitational and elastic potential energy, kinetic energy, the Work–Energy Theorem, and mechanical energy conservation. Thermal energy, heat transfer, temperature, internal energy, and thermodynamics (HS-PS3-3, HS-PS3-4) are intentionally reserved for **Unit 5**. Friction in Unit 3 is modeled strictly as negative mechanical work (`W_f = -F_f · d`) that reduces a system's mechanical energy.

---

## 🎯 Unit Overview & Pedagogical Progression

Students transition from forces and kinematics into an energy-accounting framework for physical systems. Rather than tracking instantaneous acceleration at every point along curved ramps, springs, or roller coasters, students learn to measure **Work** (`W = F · d`), **Power** (`P = W / t`), **Gravitational Potential Energy** (`PE_g = m · g · h`), **Elastic Potential Energy** (`PE_s = ½ · k · x²`), and **Kinetic Energy** (`KE = ½ · m · v²`) to predict speeds, heights, stopping distances, and system constraints.

### Essential Questions (EQs)
1. **EQ1 (Work & Power):** Why does carrying a heavy backpack across a flat hallway require biological stamina yet count as zero mechanical work in physics, while running up stairs takes the same mechanical work as walking yet demands greater power?
2. **EQ2 (Stored Energy):** How do height, spring stiffness, and stretch distance store measurable capacity to launch objects or cause motion?
3. **EQ3 (Motion & Stopping Distance):** Why does doubling a car's speed require four times the braking distance instead of twice the braking distance?
4. **EQ4 (System Accounting):** How can an energy balance sheet (`PE_i + KE_i + W_ext = PE_f + KE_f`) predict final speed and maximum hill height on any roller coaster without calculating forces along curved tracks?

### Class Differentiation Framework
- **Periods 1–3 (Conceptual Physics):** Emphasize qualitative LOL energy bar charts, proportional data tables (doubling mass doubles KE; doubling speed quadruples KE), and visual/experimental ramp measurements using g = 10 m/s² without square-root algebra or trigonometry.
- **Periods 4–6 (CP Physics):** Apply stacked single-formula problem solving (`PE_g = m · g · h`, `KE = ½ · m · v²`, `W = F · d`), structured calculator steps, and guided unit verification (`J`, `W`, `N/m`, `m/s`).
- **Period 0 (Honors Physics):** Derive speed from energy conservation (`v = √(2 · g · Δh)`), account for non-conservative negative work by friction (`W_f = -F_f · d`), work with `W = F · d · cos(θ)` for angled pulls, and linearize squared experimental graphs (`PE_s` vs. `x²`, `KE` vs. `v²`).
