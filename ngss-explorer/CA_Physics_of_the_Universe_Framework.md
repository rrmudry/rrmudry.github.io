## Introduction

Physical processes govern everything in the universe. Geoscientists require a strong background in the laws of physics in order to interpret processes that shape the Earth system `[CCC-4]`, and physicists benefit from applying their models `[SEP-2]` in a range of contexts. Forces of moving water push tiny particles of sand along beds of rivers, sometimes hard enough that they collide with the rocks with such force that a piece of the river bed breaks off. Over time, the Grand Canyon forms. Gravity pulls constantly on rocks at the surface of the Earth, and sometimes the frictional forces resisting movement falter. A landslide crashes down a canyon, destroying everything in its path. The nuclei of atoms thousands of miles below the surface that have remained stable for millions of years spontaneously explode apart, releasing massive amounts of energy `[CCC-5]` and heating up the surrounding rock. A geyser of hot steam erupts in California, releasing some of this excess heat to the surface. In each case, an Earth or space scientist is studying the physics of the situation, perhaps using a computer model to fast forward millions of years of energy `[CCC-5]` transfer to explain `[SEP-6]` what we see on Earth today. Alongside this scientist is a team of engineers, hoping to use this understanding to design and test solutions to many of society’s problems from natural hazards to global warming, or to minimize our impact on the natural world.

Physics teachers may not have a strong Earth science background. While it is true that there may be historical background and details that are new, the physical processes are not. The laws of physics are universal. In fact, Earth and space science applications are excellent motivations to the study of physical laws. A classic example is waves, a topic with such universal importance that the California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS) devotes an entire set of disciplinary core ideas (DCIs) in physical science to them.

## Instructional Segments (IS) Overview

The CA NGSS 3-Course Model for **Physics of the Universe** divides the high school course into six core Instructional Segments:

### 1. **IS1: Forces and Motion**
- **Guiding Questions**:
  - How can Newton’s laws be used to explain how and why things move?
  - How can mathematical models of Newton’s laws be used to test and improve engineering designs?
- **Performance Expectations**: `HS-PS2-1`, `HS-PS2-2`, `HS-PS2-3`, `HS-ETS1-1`, `HS-ETS1-2`, `HS-ETS1-3`, `HS-ETS1-4`
- **Key Concepts**: Newton's Laws ($F=ma$), Impulse and Momentum ($F\Delta t = m\Delta v$), vehicle crash crumple zones, seismic deformation squeeze box models.

### 2. **IS2: Forces at a Distance**
- **Guiding Questions**:
  - How can different objects interact when they are not even touching?
  - How do interactions between matter at the microscopic scale affect the macroscopic properties of matter that we observe?
  - How do satellites stay in orbit?
- **Performance Expectations**: `HS-PS2-4`, `HS-PS2-6`, `HS-ESS1-4`
- **Key Concepts**: Inverse-Square Law geometry, Newton's Law of Gravitation, Coulomb's Law ($F = k q_1 q_2 / r^2$), Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion, molecular-level structure of conductors/insulators.

### 3. **IS3: Energy Conversion and Renewable Energy**
- **Guiding Questions**:
  - How do power plants generate electricity?
  - What engineering designs can help increase the efficiency of our electricity production and reduce the negative impacts of using fossil fuels?
- **Performance Expectations**: `HS-PS2-5`, `HS-PS3-1`, `HS-PS3-2`, `HS-PS3-3`, `HS-PS3-5`, `HS-PS4-5`, `HS-ESS3-2`, `HS-ESS3-3`, `HS-ETS1-1` through `HS-ETS1-4`
- **Key Concepts**: Conservation of energy, First Law of Thermodynamics, energy transformations, electrical generators, magnetic induction, solar cells, power plant fuel flow modeling, environmental cost-benefit analysis.

### 4. **IS4: Nuclear Processes**
- **Guiding Questions**:
  - What is the internal structure of atoms and how does it release nuclear energy?
  - How do scientists use radioactive decay to determine the history and age of Earth?
- **Performance Expectations**: `HS-PS1-8`, `HS-ESS1-5`, `HS-ESS1-6`, `HS-ESS2-1`
- **Key Concepts**: Fission, fusion ($E=mc^2$), alpha/beta/gamma decay, radiometric dating of meteorites, Earth's age (4.54 billion years), plate tectonics seafloor age gradients.

### 5. **IS5: Waves and Electromagnetic Radiation**
- **Guiding Questions**:
  - How do waves transfer energy and information without transferring matter?
  - How do scientists use waves to probe Earth's interior and transmit digital data?
- **Performance Expectations**: `HS-PS4-1`, `HS-PS4-2`, `HS-PS4-3`, `HS-PS4-4`, `HS-ESS2-3`
- **Key Concepts**: Wave equation ($v = f\lambda$), P-wave and S-wave seismic shadow zones, digital vs. analog transmission, wave-particle duality, photon energy ($E=hf$), EM radiation absorption hazards.

### 6. **IS6: Stars and the Origin of the Universe**
- **Guiding Questions**:
  - How do stars produce energy and forge elements throughout their life cycles?
  - What evidence supports the Big Bang theory and the expansion of the universe?
- **Performance Expectations**: `HS-ESS1-1`, `HS-ESS1-2`, `HS-ESS1-3`
- **Key Concepts**: Nuclear fusion in stars, Hertzsprung-Russell (H-R) diagrams, stellar nucleosynthesis (elements up to iron and supernova heavy elements), Doppler redshift of galaxy spectra, Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation (CMBR).
