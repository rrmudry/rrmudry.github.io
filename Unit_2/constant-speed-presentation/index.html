Foundational Concept

What Exactly IS Speed?

In everyday language, "speed" means fast. In physics, speed is a precise rate of travel.

  • Scalar Quantity: Speed tells you how fast an object moves, regardless of its direction.
  • Physical Meaning: Speed is the distance covered in one single unit of time (e.g. each 1 second or each 1 hour).
  • Constant Speed: An object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time without speeding up or slowing down.

Analogy: The Ticking Meter Stick

If a wind-up beetle travels at 5 cm/s:

Second 1 → Travels 5 cm
Second 2 → Travels another 5 cm (10 cm total)
Second 3 → Travels another 5 cm (15 cm total)

It never accelerates. It churns out 5 cm during every second on the clock.

Formula 1 of 3

Calculating Speed: v = d / t

When you know Distance and Time:
v = d / t

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Standard Units in Motion

m/s (Meters per Second): Standard SI unit for physics laboratory problems.
cm/s (Centimeters per Second): Used for tabletop inquiry (like our 20.0 cm wind-up toys!).
km/h or mph: Used for road vehicles and long-distance navigation.
The Algebraic Wheel

The "Magic" Formula Triangle

You never need to memorize three separate equations! The Formula Triangle holds all three relationships in one visual diagram:

  • Distance (d) lives on top because it is the numerator.
  • Speed (v) and Time (t) sit on the bottom side-by-side.
  • The horizontal line means DIVIDE (÷).
  • The vertical line means MULTIPLY (·).
💡 The Rule of Thumb: Cover the variable you want to find with your thumb. The remaining letters reveal the formula!
Formula 2 of 3

Solving for Distance: d = v · t

When you know Speed and Time:
d = v · t

Distance = Speed × Time

Worked Example: The Toy Beetle

A wind-up toy beetle walks at a steady speed of 4.5 cm/s. How far does it travel in 6.0 seconds?

Given: v = 4.5 cm/s, t = 6.0 s
Formula: d = v · t
Substitute: d = (4.5 cm/s) · (6.0 s)
Answer: d = 27.0 cm

Notice the seconds (s) cancel out, leaving centimeters (cm)!

Formula 3 of 3

Solving for Time: t = d / v

When you know Distance and Speed:
t = d / v

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Worked Example: Crossing the Classroom

A motorized tumble buggy travels at 0.25 m/s. How much time does it need to cross a 3.0 meter lab track?

Given: d = 3.0 m, v = 0.25 m/s
Formula: t = d / v
Substitute: t = 3.0 m / 0.25 m/s
Answer: t = 12.0 seconds

Dividing 3 by 0.25 is the same as asking: "How many quarters are in $3.00?" (12!).

Key Takeaways & Today's Mission

The Constant Motion Triad

1. Speed: v = d / t (Divide distance by time)
2. Distance: d = v · t (Multiply speed by time)
3. Time: t = d / v (Divide distance by speed)
