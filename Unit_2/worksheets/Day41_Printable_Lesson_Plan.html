Physics Lesson Plan: Day 41

Unit 2 // Free Fall Foundations & 2D Vector Independence
Date: Monday, October 26, 2026
Period Duration: 50 Minutes
Standards: NGSS HS-PS2-1, SEP-5
⚠️ Non-Negotiable Points You Must Emphasize in Class
  • Earth's Gravity Standard: We always use g = 10 m/s² (downward). Every second in free fall adds 10 m/s of downward speed (v = 10 · t).
  • Mass Does Not Matter in Free Fall: Heavy and light objects fall at the exact same rate when air resistance is removed. (Demonstrate with paper on textbook).
  • Perpendicular Independence (The Golden Rule): Gravity pulls only downward. Horizontal forward speed never pulls upward and never keeps an object in the air longer.
  • The Free Fall Bridge: Horizontal projectiles are just free falling objects that happen to be coasting sideways. The vertical drop time (t) dictates how long the object has to travel horizontally (Δx = vx · t).
Stage 1: Bell-Ringer & Concept Prediction 7 Minutes
Prompt on Whiteboard / Screen:

"A heavy metal ball is dropped from a 1.0 meter table. At the exact same second, an identical ball is shot horizontally off the table at 15 m/s. Which ball hits the floor first? (A) Dropped ball, (B) Fired ball, or (C) Same time?"

Teacher Action: Take a quick visual poll (finger votes: 1, 2, or 3) before revealing the answer. Note that 70–80% of students will incorrectly vote for (B), believing horizontal speed "carries" the ball longer. Do not give away the answer yet—pivot directly to Stage 2.
Stage 2: Free Fall Foundations & Mythbusting Demos 13 Minutes
Demo A: Air Resistance vs. Free Fall (3 min)
  • Hold a flat sheet of paper in one hand and a heavy textbook in the other. Drop them simultaneously: the book crashes down, the paper flutters slowly.
  • The Reveal: Place the flat paper directly on top of the book. Drop them together. The paper stays pinned to the book all the way down because the book moves the air out of the way!
  • Core Takeaway: In true free fall (without air resistance), mass has zero effect on falling acceleration.
The Simple g = 10 Math Pattern (4 min) — Write this on the board:
  • Speed equation: v = 10 · t  (t = 0s → 0 m/s; t = 1s → 10 m/s; t = 2s → 20 m/s; t = 3s → 30 m/s).
  • Distance fallen: Δy = ½ · g · t² = 5 · t²  (1s drops 5m; 2s drops 20m; 3s drops 45m).
Demo B: The "Drop vs. Fire" Live Showdown (6 min)
  • Use the spring-loaded tabletop launcher (or a ruler flicking one coin horizontally while dropping another off the edge).
  • Tell the class: "Close your eyes and listen to the floor impact."
  • Auditory Proof: They hear only one single click (*clack*). Both hit the floor at the exact same instant!
Stage 3: Interactive Visualizer & Strobe Comparison 12 Minutes
Open Tool: rrmudry.github.io/horizontal-projectile-motion/ (on classroom projector)
Point out the two strobe axes on screen:
1. Cliff wall marks (Red vertical ticks): Spacing grows wider and wider as the ball falls. That is downward acceleration (g = 10 m/s²).
2. Ground marks (Blue horizontal ticks): Spacing between ticks is completely identical. That is constant horizontal speed (ax = 0).
Slider Investigation with Students:
  • Keep height at 50 m. Launch at 10 m/s → Time = 3.16 s, Range = 31.6 m.
  • Double speed to 20 m/s → Time = 3.16 s (UNCHANGED!), Range = 63.2 m.
  • Quadruple speed to 40 m/s → Time = 3.16 s (STILL UNCHANGED!), Range = 126.5 m.
  • Ask students: "What is the ONLY slider that can change how many seconds the ball stays in the air?" → Initial Height!
Stage 4: The 2-Column (X vs. Y) Problem Setup 15 Minutes
Have students draw this standard T-Chart in their notebooks:
HORIZONTAL AXIS (X) VERTICAL AXIS (Y)
• Motion: Constant Speed
• Acceleration: ax = 0 m/s²
• Formula: Δx = vx · t 		• Motion: Free Fall (Gravity)
• Acceleration: g = 10 m/s² (down)
• Initial vertical speed: v0y = 0 m/s
• Formula: Δy = 5 · t²  →  t = √(Δy / 5)
Model This Problem Together on the Board:

"A marble rolls off a lab counter that is 1.25 meters high with a horizontal speed of 2.0 m/s. How far from the counter base does it land?"

  1. Step 1 (Vertical Free Fall): Find time to fall.
    Δy = 5 · t²  →  1.25 = 5 · t²  →  t² = 1.25 / 5 = 0.25  →  t = 0.50 seconds
  2. Step 2 (Horizontal Coasting): Find landing distance.
    Δx = vx · t  →  Δx = (2.0 m/s) · (0.50 s)  →  Δx = 1.0 meter
Stage 5: Exit Check & Wrap-Up 3 Minutes
Thumbs Up / Thumbs Down Rapid Check:
  1. "If I double table height, does flight time increase?" (Thumbs UP - Yes).
  2. "If I shoot the ball twice as fast horizontally, does flight time increase?" (Thumbs DOWN - No, only horizontal distance increases).
  3. "Preview of Wednesday/Thursday Block Lab:" We will calculate where a marble will land off the table and place a target cup on the floor for a blind live trial!
