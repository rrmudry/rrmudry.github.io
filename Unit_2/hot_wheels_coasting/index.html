⚖️ Stage 3: The "Wait, Does Weight Help or Hurt?" Challenge
15 Mins • Conceptual Hook
🤔 The Big Conceptual Question (Choose BEFORE testing!):

"If I take our best-rolling car (Champion Car) and tape pennies to its roof, it presses down harder on the floor (more downward force).

Will it stop sooner? Or will its extra momentum keep it rolling farther?"

Hypothesis A: It will stop SOONER (shorter distance).

Heavier car = presses down harder = more friction forces opposing motion, slowing the car down faster.

Hypothesis B: It will roll FARTHER (greater distance).

Heavier car = more mass = more momentum and kinetic energy to plow through rolling resistance.

📉 Stage 4: Understanding Deceleration (Slowing Down)
Conceptual Physics • Why Do Cars Stop?

Why do Hot Wheels cars slow down and stop across the floor? As your car rolls along the flat floor, no engine or hand is pushing it forward. The floor pushes backward with friction against the spinning wheels!

1. Moving Forward vs. Pushing Backward

Because friction pushes in the opposite direction of the car's motion, it acts like an invisible brake, stealing speed every second until the car stops.

2. Why Physicists Call This "Negative Acceleration"
Positive Acceleration (+):
Speeding up in the forward direction (like when rolling down the launch ramp!).
Negative Acceleration (−) / Deceleration:
Slowing down while moving forward. The backward push makes the speed decrease toward zero.
💡 Key takeaway: You don't have to be moving backwards to have negative acceleration! Whenever you are slowing down in a forward direction, your acceleration is negative.
🎞️ Visualizing Slowing Down: Strobe Dot Trail

Imagine a dripping paint dropper attached to your car dropping 1 dot every 0.1 seconds onto the floor. Notice the spacing between the dots:

[0 cm Bottom of Ramp] ●●●● (Fast: Dots Far Apart) ●●● ●● ●●● (Slowing Down: Dots Crowd Closer) [CAR STOPS ●]
Because the car covers less distance each second as it slows down, the dots get closer and closer together until it stops.
