📚 The 3 Categories of Inertia: Framework & Real-World Anchors

Category Fundamental Physics Meaning The Everyday Physical Sensation Classic Real-World Demonstrations
1. Inertia of Rest An object at rest maintains zero velocity unless acted on by an external net force (F_net ≠ 0). The object feels "left behind" because the base or surface moved out from underneath it before friction could grab it. • Rapidly whipping a silk tablecloth from under a stack of dishes.
• Snapping an index card out from over a beaker so a penny drops straight in.
• Jerking backward in your seat when a subway train or bus suddenly accelerates forward.
2. Inertia of Motion An object traveling at constant velocity maintains that speed indefinitely. No forward force exists to sustain it. The vehicle or ground stops, but your body continues traveling forward at the initial speed until a stopping force acts on it. • A passenger without a seatbelt colliding with the dashboard during a sudden stop.
• Stumbling forward when your skateboard or feet trip over a sidewalk crack.
• Shaking water droplets off wet hands (hands stop, water droplets keep flying).
3. Inertia of Direction Moving objects travel in a straight line. Curving or turning requires a continuous perpendicular inward force. The vehicle turns, but your body wants to travel in a straight line, making you feel "pressed" against the outside car door. • Releasing a whirled tetherball: it flies along a straight tangent line, NOT a spiral.
• Water droplets spraying off a spinning bicycle wheel in straight tangent arcs.
• A car sliding off an icy mountain curve in a straight line tangent to the turn.
