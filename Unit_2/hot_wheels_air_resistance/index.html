🤔 Stage 2: The Wind Speed & Stopping Distance Prediction
Conceptual Hypothesis
💨 The Core Inquiry Question:

"As we increase the fan speed from OFF (0) → LOW (1) → MEDIUM (2) → HIGH (3), what will happen to the distance your air-brake car travels before stopping, and how will the backward net force change?"

Hypothesis A: Higher fan speed = Much SHORTER distance (Stops Sooner).

Faster wind slams into the index card with greater force. The backward drag force adds to rolling friction, creating a larger backward net force that decelerates the car faster.

Hypothesis B: Distance will remain nearly unchanged.

The car's momentum rolling down the ramp is too heavy for fan air to make a noticeable difference in stopping distance.

Hypothesis C: Higher fan speed = LONGER distance.

Turbulent air swirls around the sides of the card and pushes the car forward like an aerodynamic slipstream.

📉 Stage 4: Free-Body Diagram & Net Deceleration Force
Newton's 2nd Law • F_net = m × a

Why did the car stop in a much shorter distance as the fan speed increased? Because two backward forces combined to resist the car's forward motion!

1. Two Backward Forces: Friction + Air Drag

On the flat runway, the total backward force is F_backward = F_friction + F_air_drag. When the fan blows harder, F_air_drag skyrockets, producing a larger backward net force!

2. Newton's Second Law: a = F_net / m
Fan OFF (Setting 0):
F_net is small (friction only) → gentle deceleration → longest stopping distance.
Fan MEDIUM (Setting 2):
F_net increases → sharper deceleration rate → moderate stopping distance.
Fan HIGH (Setting 3):
F_net is maximum → violent braking deceleration → shortest stopping distance!
