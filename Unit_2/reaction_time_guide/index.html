Average Formula

Convert Centimeters to Meters (cm ➔ m)

In physics, gravitational acceleration is in m/s². You cannot plug centimeters into kinematic formulas—distance must be in meters!

SI Unit Conversion Rule
Distance in Meters (x) =
Distance (cm) 100
There are exactly 100 centimeters in 1 meter. Move the decimal point 2 places to the left (← ←).
1 Identify Decimal
19.5 cm
Locate the decimal point in your average centimeters.
2 Divide by 100
19.5 cm 100
= 0.195 m
Jump the decimal 2 places left (divide by 100).
3 Record in Meters
x = 0.195 m
Always include the leading zero to prevent misreading.

Calculate Reaction Time: t = √(x / 5)

We use g = 10 m/s² (or -10 m/s²) for gravity. Starting from rest (v₀ = 0), free fall simplifies to x = ½gt² = ½(10)t² = 5t². Solving for time gives:

Classroom Free-Fall Formula (g = 10 m/s²)
t =
x 5
Where x = drop distance in meters, and 5 comes from ½ · 10 m/s².
1 Divide by 5
0.195 m 5
= 0.039
Divide your distance in meters (x) by 5.
2 Square Root (√)
0.039
= 0.197 s
Press the square root key. Round to 3 decimal places.
3 Record in Table
t = 0.197 s
Write in the Reaction Time (s) column on your lab paper.

Analysis Questions & CER Framework

Synthesize your quantitative findings into scientific arguments supported by evidence and neurobiology principles.

1 Fastest vs Slowest
Compare Baseline Times
Identify who had the lowest reaction time in seconds and discuss physiological reasons.
2 Distraction Impact
Δt = t(dist) - t(undis)
Calculate the increase in reaction time caused by conversation or mobile device use.
3 Real-World Driving
Δd = v · Δt
At 60 mph (27 m/s), an extra 0.1 s delay adds 2.7 meters (9 feet) of blind travel!

Exemplar CER Model (Claim, Evidence, Reasoning)

Claim
Auditory and cognitive distractions significantly increase human reaction time during visual motor tasks.
Evidence
In our group, average reaction time increased from 0.184 s (undistracted, ~17.0 cm) to 0.228 s (distracted, ~25.9 cm), representing an average reflex delay of +0.044 s (+44 ms). On multiple distracted attempts, participants caught the ruler past 28 cm, right near the 30 cm ruler edge!
Reasoning
Because attention is finite, processing conversational input creates cognitive bottlenecks in the prefrontal cortex, delaying the transmission of neural motor impulses from the visual cortex to the fingers.
