Teacher & Student Lab Guide: Sinusoidal Sound Modeling

Vernier Logger Pro Alternative for Chromebooks & Desmos

Is a Chromebook / Phone Microphone Sufficient?

YES! Built-in microphones on Chromebooks and smartphones sample physical audio at 44,100 to 48,000 measurements per second (44.1–48 kHz). For a standard 440 Hz tuning fork, a single sound wave cycle lasts only 0.00227 seconds (2.27 milliseconds) and contains over 100 distinct data points!

Why existing apps often failed: By default, Chrome enables voice noise cancellation, echo suppression, and automatic gain control. These algorithms mistake the pure tone of a tuning fork for "steady background noise" and filter it out. Sound Wave Studio explicitly bypasses these filters to capture the raw acoustic wave directly from the physical microphone capsule!

Step-by-Step Classroom Lab Protocol

  1. Strike the Tuning Fork: Strike the fork firmly against a rubber strike pad, rubber sole of a shoe, or knee. Never strike a tuning fork against hard desks or metal, as this produces non-sinusoidal high-frequency clangs that distort the fundamental wave.
  2. Position near Microphone: Hold the vibrating tines about 2 to 4 inches (5–10 cm) away from your Chromebook or laptop's microphone pinhole.
  3. Capture the Wave: Click Start Live Mic. Once you see the wave oscillating on screen, click Freeze Wave (or enable Auto-Trigger, which will automatically freeze the wave as soon as you strike the fork!).
  4. Use Calipers to Measure Wave Dimensions:
    • Drag Caliper 1 to a wave crest (peak), and Caliper 2 to the very next crest.
    • The difference in time is the Period T: T = t₂ - t₁.
    • Calculate Frequency: f = 1 / T (should closely match the number stamped on your tuning fork, e.g. 256 Hz, 440 Hz, 512 Hz!).
    • Calculate Angular Frequency: B = 2π / T = 2π × f.
    • Measure Peak-to-Peak height to find Amplitude A: A = (y_max - y_min) / 2.
    • Find Midline D: D = (y_max + y_min) / 2.
  5. Write Your Mathematical Model: Formulate your equation: y = A · sin(B(t - C)) + D (or use Cosine form!). Type your parameters into the Modeling Workbench or click ⚡ Auto-Set from Calipers to see if your theoretical mathematical curve aligns with your physical sound wave!
  6. Transfer to Desmos: Click 📋 Copy Table for Desmos, open Desmos, and press Ctrl + V. Type y_1 ~ a*sin(b*(x_1 - c)) + d to see Desmos calculate the statistical regression curve!
